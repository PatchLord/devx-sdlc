## Required changes

**1. §10.4 — delete or rebuild the "model prompt" promise. Blocking.**
> "that field never reaches our application logs, never reaches an error report, and never enters a model prompt."

§10.2, nine hundred words earlier, says we have "no removal of personal data from prompts and session transcripts **as a mechanism rather than a habit**." So the document promises as an accomplished fact the exact control it elsewhere admits does not exist. The logs half is real (allowlist plus a test). The error-report half is unverified — error payloads go to a third-party tracker and nothing in §7 asserts the allowlist covers them. The prompt half is a habit. A client reads all three at the same strength. Rewrite to: logs — mechanism, with the test named as an artefact; error reports — same mechanism, criterion added; prompts — **stated as an instruction to named people, not a mechanism, and closed by the engagement data-handling note (D-5).** This sentence, unchanged, is the one I would be apologising for.

**2. S-1 cannot happen on the dates given without breaching §10.1. Blocking.**
§10.1: "Nothing of Marigold's — not a document, not a line of code, **not a store response** — is uploaded until Dev has replied in writing that the annexe is acceptable." S-1 measures D-1 against "roughly 90 real UK orders" by **14 August**. D-5's note is drafted **18 August**. C-7's written reply is "before any upload" with no date. A Shopify orders read returns customer names, emails and shipping addresses — so the first thing this engagement does is handle the client's customer personal data, four days before the policy exists, in the phase most likely to involve an agent session. And §10.4's table says customer data is "**Not ingested**", which is true of the board and false of discovery. Fix all three: state that S-1 handles customer personal data and that the board does not; make C-7 and the D-5 note **preconditions of S-1, dated ahead of 14 August**; and if D-1's measurement is done by hand with no agent session, say so and name who does it.

**3. Phase 1 has no authentication criterion. Blocking.**
§5.1 lists "permissions and authorisation" on the protected set, and A-11 exhaustively covers role-to-action. Nothing in §7.1 asserts that **a person who is not a user sees nothing.** An internet-reachable board holding seven staff members' names, their action history and Marigold's order numbers, with no criterion for the login boundary. Add a criterion: an unauthenticated request to every route returns nothing, plus a browser test of the sign-in boundary, plus session expiry. A-11 currently begins at "everyone sees every task", which assumes the answer to the question nobody asked.

**4. The phase-2 webhook receiver is in scope, is unauthenticated as written, and is not on the protected list. Blocking.**
P2-3: "The store tells the board when something changed." A-21 proves idempotency ("a signal that arrives twice does not produce two") and **nothing proves authenticity.** A public endpoint that creates work for four people, with no signature verification criterion, no replay window, no rejection criterion for a forged payload. §5.1's protected set names the phase-2 notification path and omits the receiver. Add: the receiver to the protected list; a criterion that an unsigned or mis-signed payload is rejected and counted; a criterion for a replayed-but-valid payload outside the freshness window; and re-ask §4's four questions **at the start of phase 2** rather than carrying Standard forward, because "an endpoint anyone on the internet can post to" is a different answer to question one.

**5. Phase 1 has no criterion for the scheduled reader failing silently.**
P1-2 and P1-3 are the board's entire reason to exist and both hang off a poll. 05-depth's rule for work with no interface is explicit that the four forgotten failure modes are **criteria, not monitoring wishes**: it did not start, it processed zero rows, it ran twice, the dead-letter queue is not empty. §4.4 sells alarms as part of Standard; §7.1 contains no criterion for any of them; §5.3's Setup exit does not include monitoring; A-22 exists only in phase 2 and only for live signals. So today, if the reader dies on 3 October, the board looks calm and Marigold finds out the way they find out now. Add four criteria to §7.1 with the alarm's own test run as the artefact.

**6. §4.3 answers a different question than §4.1 asked. The depth deviation must be recorded, not argued away.**
> "High is where money moves, or where something cannot be undone. Neither is true of phase 1."

Our own table says High applies where there is "money, personal data, or cannot be undone" — three limbs, and §4.1 already answered the middle one yes: "It does hold **staff personal data** … including the reopen statistics in P1-16." §4.2 uses the personal-data limb to rule out Light and then §4.3 forgets it exists. I am willing to land on Standard — see my accepted risks — but not on a sentence that quietly drops a limb of our own test. Rewrite §4.3 to state that the personal-data limb is met, name the compensating controls (no store writes, no customer ingestion, no money, uat exists, protected-path code-owner review, allowlist logging, invented data outside production), name the deviation as a deviation, and name me as the person who accepted it.

**7. §4.3 sells High's second reviewer as operable. It has never been tried.**
> "three of the things it adds are real and we can operate them: a second environment … **a second named approver on anything touching the dangerous paths** …"

Two problems. Our own depth doc says two reviewers "is not an approval count, and cannot be" — the host has no per-path approval count, so it has to come from two owner entries; and D4 in our research says of that mechanism, "Believed yes; **never tried**." So one of High's three "we can operate them" rows is in the same evidential state as the two we declined. Either move it to *to build*, or prove it during Setup (see 9) before the sentence goes to a client. The same mechanism is what §13.4 relies on at Standard via Deepa — §5.2 is honest about that dependency; §4.3 is not.

**8. §5.1 — "cannot be back-dated" is false as written.**
> "Intent is written down before the code exists, and it cannot be back-dated."

What is enforced is **ancestry**, not chronology: the spec must be the branch's first commit and an ancestor of every code commit. Commit dates are trivially settable, and a branch that started with code can be rebased into legality — our own pre-mortem says so. The honest sentence is: "a branch where the code came first cannot merge without being rebuilt, and the rebuild is visible." §5.1's last sentence in the same section already gets this right; this one does not, and a client reads it as tamper-proof.

**9. §5.3's Setup exit needs a fourth item, and §5.1's absolute needs a status row.**
The three items are right and the third is the best idea in the document. Add: **a code-owner review actually demanded and satisfied on a protected path**, with the run link. §5.2 already names the failure — "a pattern that matches nothing is owned by nobody while still reading as protected" — and CODEOWNERS naming a slug that does not resolve blocks every merge with an unhelpful error. Until that has happened once, every protected-path claim in §5.1, §7 and §12 is decoration. Separately, "No developer or agent session holds production credentials" appears in §5.1 as an absolute with **no row in §5.2**. Give it one.

**10. §8.2 is missing the dependencies the whole perimeter sits on.**
The repository, hosting and every third-party account are Marigold's, "on Marigold's card, with Dev as owner and devx added as a member." Therefore: branch protection and rulesets depend on **their** repository plan; `perimeter` needs a fine-grained token with `Administration: read`, which some organisations block by policy; the workflows need runners available to **their** repository; the review job needs a model-provider API key on **their** account — which means Marigold's card is being charged for our review agent, and §11.3 has no line for it. None of this is in §8.2 with an owner and a date, and discovering any of it after signature is worse than discovering it in the first ten minutes. Add C-rows for all four, dated before Setup, and settle in §11 who pays for the review agent's spend.

**11. Disclose R3 — provenance in a repository Marigold owns.**
Not mentioned anywhere. Agents write most of the code and the repository is Marigold's from the first commit, so whatever we put in commit trailers is a **permanent, discoverable** record that AI authored their deliverable — in an asset they may later show a buyer, an auditor, or the next agency. That is a contracts question and it is Dev's to answer, not ours to default. It also interacts with D-6: those trailers are evidence in exactly the dispute the originality clause creates. Add a row: whether commits carry provenance, who decided, and that the decision is permanent.

**12. Disclose R6 — how an agent is used during an incident. §11.4 sells that window.**
Options A and B put devx on defect-fix duty from 8 October to 5 December with alarms routed to us. Nothing in the document says whether production logs or a production task row may enter an agent session, whether an agent may run remediation, who reads the diff at 23:00 when the reviewer is asleep, or how an AI-assisted fix is reconstructed afterwards. And §5.1 says no session holds production credentials — so the document sells a support window and forbids the access it needs, without describing the path. The D-5 note must cover incident handling, not only uploads, and §11.4 A and B must say what the fix path actually is.

**13. Disclose R4 and R7 — the perimeter lives on settings we do not own.**
§5.2 says branch protection is "a setting on Marigold's repository" and stops there. Say the consequence: Dev can turn off a required check, change protection or rotate a token, and our answer is that the `perimeter` job **reports** it — it cannot prevent it. Name who at Marigold may change repository settings, and record what we do if a gate is removed mid-phase. R7's ask is a written exception criterion so a non-conforming engagement is a recorded decision rather than a silent hole; this SOW has one such exception already (see 17) and no place to record it.

**14. A-1's artefact depends on an event X-1 forbids us from causing.**
> "a screenshot of the board taken after a real crossing on Marigold's store during the pre-window fortnight"

We are contractually read-only, so we cannot make a product cross its floor. If ~60 moving SKUs happen not to cross in that fortnight, A-1 has no artefact and the phase ends in an argument. Either add a C-row — a named person at Marigold adjusts one product's stock in the admin on a named day — or make the artefact a ledger-driven replay of a real reading sequence plus the screenshot **if** a natural crossing occurs. Same shape of problem in A-7: "one screenshot from Rakesh's actual handset taken by a named person" is a dependency on a person and a device (D-8) with no fallback.

**15. Two criteria are broader than their artefacts, and two cross-references are wrong.**
P1-1 promises the board "works on a phone … **at the signal quality of the Bhiwandi unit** — subject to A-8"; A-7 promises "Rakesh can do **a full day's work**" against "the device profile from A-8". A-8 is the stock-floors criterion. There is no device-or-network criterion in §7 at all, and no row in §6 saying we do not verify network conditions. So the document promises behaviour on a bad connection at the unit and proves nothing about it. Either add a criterion — a browser run at a named throttled network and device profile, with the profile written down — or move it to §6 as a named non-verification. And narrow A-7's wording to what its artefact shows: sees the board, completes his own task, on profile X.
